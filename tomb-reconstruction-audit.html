Reconstructing Tutankhamun’s tomb

Working reconstruction, not an exact archaeological simulation. Excavation findspots, ancient burial arrangements, and modern visitor views are different states. The walkthrough uses documented rooms and approximate positions, with exposed examination copies for objects originally concealed. Missing objects are listed below. Plain modern teaching ramps bridge floor drops at the burial doorway and Annexe. These are access aids, not ancient features.

Plan and excavation photograph references

Room layout and surfaces

Carter’s tomb notes and measurements are the baseline. Only the burial chamber was painted. Other rooms need rough, tool-marked limestone rather than decorative hieroglyphic wallpaper. The burial chamber ceiling was unfinished rock.

For higher fidelity, obtain four rectified color wall images with approved reuse rights, then align them to the wall dimensions and door/niche masks. Add restrained plaster/rock roughness and normal maps; do not make the paint metallic. Use separate ceiling and floor materials. Keep readable wall images on mobile rather than compressing all four into one small atlas.

The Factum high-resolution wall viewer is the strongest color and surface reference located. Its facsimile documentation also records the removed south-wall portion. Reference access does not itself provide permission to redistribute those image files. They are linked here, not copied into this site.

Getty’s conservation documentation compares modern recording with historical photographs. Use that comparison to separate age, repairs and conservation traces from any proposed ancient appearance. A brighter speculative restoration should be a separately labeled option, never silently substituted for evidence.

Missing assets, in priority order

  1. Burial chamber: four nested gilded shrines and the proper three-coffin assembly. These occupied much of the chamber; leaving them out makes the room look too empty and exposes walls differently.
  2. Antechamber: two guardian statues at the burial doorway, three animal-headed funerary couches, disassembled chariots, stacked chests and vessels. Reproduce the documented crowding with low-detail background models while preserving navigable teaching routes.
  3. Treasury: the separated Anubis replica now supplies a study stand-in near the entrance, but its carrying poles are missing. An accurate full shrine, canopic canopy and four protective goddesses, plus documented chests and shrine groups are still needed.
  4. Annexe: furniture, containers, vessels and other recorded contents. Start with broad categories and consult a numbered excavation plan before assigning exact objects.
  5. Walls and architecture: higher-resolution licensed wall images, missing south-wall section, accurate wall openings, door closures, floor drops, niches, tool-marked limestone and undecorated ceilings.

The missing groups are a reconstruction worklist, not a claim that usable free meshes have already been found. Download candidates and provenance checks →

Validation before calling it historically accurate

  • Compare a top-down mesh plan and floor levels with Carter’s plan; verify the Treasury/Annexe labels and each doorway.
  • Check every rendered wall upright, unmirrored, at the correct compass orientation; use north/east/south/west names rather than ambiguous “left” and “back.”
  • Separate documented room, approximate model coordinate, teaching offset, replica identity and unknown placement in the object data.
  • Test pickup and return without shifting the recorded anchor; keep the student’s held copy separate from the historical explanation.
  • Check landscape and stereo performance on the actual iPhone 14, head-motion permission, and every Pro Controller mapping. Browser tests cannot substitute for that physical device check.
