Double-tap forward: tap, release, then press forward again within 0.3 seconds and hold for 2× speed. Release to walk normally. Works with touch, W, left stick or D-pad; ZL precision movement overrides the boost. Touch: drag the scene to look around; use the movement arrows and Pick up button. Keyboard: WASD moves, arrow keys look, E picks up/returns, Q puts back, Space jumps, C crouches / stands, R returns to start.

Cardboard: choose Cardboard view, allow motion access on the iPhone, then place the phone in the viewer horizontally. Look at a nearby object and press A to pick it up. Stay seated; movement is controlled by the stick.

Adjust for your viewer. Phone/browser motion support and controller mappings vary; use landscape view if head tracking is unavailable.

About this space

The tomb is a digital reconstruction by akurtulus. Object placement follows documented rooms where possible. Teaching stands expose burial objects that were originally concealed inside coffins and wrappings. The tomb’s modern wall images show aged surfaces, not proven original colors. Teaching ramps bridge floor drops at the burial chamber and Annexe; these are modern access aids. Crouch to pass low doorways. Jump to reach ledges about one meter high when there is headroom. Walk or jump off an edge to drop and land below; ramps remain optional access aids. Picking up an object shows a scaled digital copy; returning it restores its exhibit position. The combined souvenir display has been separated: its coffin sits inside the stone sarcophagus, with the head west, and its Anubis shrine is near the Treasury entrance, also facing west. Both are replicas. Its unidentified chair/vessel component is retained only as reference. The pectoral is on a modern study stand; its exact findspot is unverified.

Outdoor approach

Outside opens Nate Loper’s Valley of the Kings terrain. Walk through the marked shelter to enter the tomb’s stairs and corridor. Valley overview switches to an aerial view. The shelter and entrance steps are illustrative connectors. An unnavigable roof section of the source entrance is opened for the new steps; this is a teaching adaptation, not a surveyed reconstruction. the terrain and interior are independently modeled spaces. Its placement is visually aligned near the KV62/KV9 approach west of the rest area using the Theban Mapping Project survey; the landscape has no geographic metadata, so the marker is approximate, not a surveyed fit.

Terrain: Valley of the Kings Tombs – Luxor, Egypt by Nate Loper · CC BY 4.0. Original mesh and 8192×4096 texture dimensions retained; image JPEG-compressed.
